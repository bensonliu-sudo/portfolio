Case study · 01 · Risk intelligence platform

FRIS risk intelligence

A risk-intelligence platform that monitors around 220 asset managers. It gathers news, regulatory releases and filings from eight sources, has an LLM classify every development into 13 risk categories on a 20-point scale, and turns them into a deterministic, explainable risk score for each entity.

The problem

Early warning, buried
in thousands of articles.

Analysts who oversee fund managers on behalf of clients need to know early when a manager runs into trouble: redemptions gated, an investor class action, a regulator's probe. The signals are spread across thousands of articles a week, in many sources and several languages.

Most of it is noise. The same story is syndicated a dozen times, law-firm press releases make routine investigations sound alarming, and news about a parent company is not always news about its local subsidiary. A keyword alert drowns; a person cannot read it all.

FRIS turns that stream into one number per entity that an analyst can defend: where it came from, which development set it, and what would bring it down.

What I built

From raw news to a score
you can defend.

01 · INGESTION AND DEDUPLICATION

Eight sources, one clean event stream

Collectors pull from news terminals, Google News, Yahoo Finance, SEC EDGAR and four regulators' releases. Full text is extracted where it can be, and duplicates are removed before anything is scored.

  • Deduplication by URL, title similarity and content hash, then 7-day semantic clustering into incidents
  • A representative article per incident, chosen by content and source authority
  • Parent-to-subsidiary propagation that judges whether a parent's news really applies
02 · AI JUDGES, ARITHMETIC SCORES

The LLM classifies; it never sets the score

Each development is classified by an LLM into one of 13 categories on a 20-point scale, under explicit consistency rules. The entity's score is then pure arithmetic: the highest category, with cooling over time and credit for mitigations.

  • Law-firm solicitation releases capped, a 33-firm blacklist, repeat alerts merged
  • Category scores cool after 60 days, or 180 while a legal matter is pending
  • The waterline shows every move, with one marker per assessed development
03 · EXPLAINABLE BY CONSTRUCTION

Every rating explains itself

The category radar shows what is driving the score today. The written analysis is generated from the scorer's own record, not free-form text, so every sentence traces back to a scored development.

  • Rating, risk profile, trajectory, last 90 days, mitigation, open matters
  • The same text goes into the PDF report for clients
04 · BUILT FOR CLIENT WORK

Rankings, watchlists, reports

A multi-layer ranking across all entities, client watchlists, regional and asset-class filters, a top-movers view, PDF export, and sign-in so clients see only their own watchlist.

How it was built

Most of the work is
removing noise.

Keep AI out of the arithmetic

The model classifies and explains; the score is a deterministic function of the classified record. That makes every rating reproducible and every change attributable.

Calibrate the judge against a gold set

A hand-labelled gold standard and regression runs check the classifier after every prompt or taxonomy change, so improvements are measured rather than assumed.

Design for the noise you actually see

Syndication, law-firm solicitations, parent and subsidiary confusion and multi-language duplicates each got a specific, tested rule.

Make it operable

One command runs the weekly update end to end, backs up the database and writes the change log, so the system does not depend on its author being there.

Scope and limits
  • A monitoring signal for analysts, not investment advice or a credit rating.
  • Coverage depends on what sources publish; paywalls limit full text for some articles.
  • The recording runs the real frontend against a demo backend: every manager, client, headline and figure in it is fictional, and the employer's name is removed.
