Case study · 02 · Data terminal and trading system

WALD data & trading

WALD is my own platform for US equities, built for production and currently in development. A data terminal turns market, filing, news and industry data into versioned, point-in-time datasets behind one read-only API and a terminal-style workspace, with industry-chain analysis at its core. A trading system on the same data uses an LLM as its analyst and code for forecasting, sizing, independent risk and execution, while collection and paper-broker services run around the clock in the cloud.

The problem

What was known, when,
and who it reaches.

Trading on events means answering three questions fast: what just happened, what was already known, and which companies it reaches. The answers sit in a dozen sources with different clocks: exchange data, SEC filings, news wires, macro releases, foreign disclosures.

Most of the errors are quiet. A restated quarter or a renamed ticker leaks information from the future into a backtest; a filing time recorded in the wrong time zone shifts a whole replay by hours.

And the most valuable signal is often one step removed: a supplier's contract, a memory price, a capacity commitment. Seeing it needs a supply chain mapped from primary evidence, not a list of tickers.

What I built

One platform,
two products, one cloud.

01 · POINT-IN-TIME DATA

Every row knows when it became known

Raw files land in an append-only lake, become fixed-version products, then a PostgreSQL database where every row carries the time it became available. Any page, API call or script can be replayed as of a past date and sees only what was known then.

  • Row-level security enforces the cutoff in the database, not in the page
  • Restatements kept as versions; ticker, CIK and FIGI resolved by date
  • Strict mode refuses a historical request it cannot honour instead of silently degrading
02 · INDUSTRY-CHAIN ANALYSIS

The AI supply chain, stage by stage

A map of AI infrastructure from hyperscalers down to chips, packaging, power and cooling: 33 stages and 171 companies, each one clickable through to its own supply chain, filings and financials.

  • Any company's suppliers and customers, five tiers up and down
  • Peers, orders, partnerships and investments as separate relation types
  • Each link marked as filed, registered or inferred
03 · EVIDENCE BEHIND EVERY LINK

Built from primary sources

The HBM workbench follows one chain in depth: materials, equipment, wafers, stacking, test, accelerators and data centres. Relationships come from filings, such as supply contracts disclosed to the Korean regulator, with their correction history kept.

  • 201 Korean supply-contract disclosures parsed, with 168 correction links
  • Product roadmaps, qualification status and capacity commitments tracked per supplier
  • A research trail runs event → product → evidence → impact
04 · TRADING SYSTEM

The model analyses; code decides

An LLM reads the filings and news and writes a research file with scenario prices by horizon. Code turns that into candidates, a manager sizes them, an independent risk station can veto, and a trader places limit orders. Every station leaves a versioned, hashed receipt.

  • Inputs unlock at the time they were received, and model inputs are scanned for leaks
  • Daily model budget enforced across all runs, with circuit breakers
  • Research memory: dated facts, live theses and expectation cards frozen before earnings
05 · CLOUD DEPLOYMENT

Running around the clock

An Azure VM has run 24/7 since 10 October 2026: 24 systemd timers poll SEC filings every minute, read news wires, record quotes, freeze and score shadow forecasts, watch every service and back up daily. A headless broker gateway connects to a paper account; live trading is never connected.

  • Hash-locked single-file deployer: checks for open positions and orders, drains running jobs, writes a receipt
  • Every deploy approved by an independent reviewer first; six in one day, including during market hours
  • Filing lake mirrored to cold cloud storage, with restores spot-checked

How it was built

Production habits,
before production.

Builder and reviewer, separated

Every work package is self-checked against the reviewer's likely attacks, then accepted by an independent AI reviewer before it is committed.

Known when, not just known

Every row carries when it became available and how that time was established. The database enforces the cutoff, so a replay cannot see the future.

Every link needs a source

A supply-chain relation is filed, registered or inferred, and says so. Inferred links never pass as disclosed ones.

The model analyses; code decides

The LLM writes research and scenarios. Sizing, risk limits, stops and orders are code, and the risk station ignores the model's opinion.

Deploys are approved and reversible

No file reaches the cloud without a recorded approval, a hash check before and after, and a receipt.

No returns claimed without evidence

Paper trading only, and three closed trades is not a track record. Profitability is the test still to pass.

Scope and limits
  • In development: the database runs on a development machine; the cloud runs collection, quotes, shadow forecasts and the paper broker. The rebuilt decision chain trades on a local paper broker and is not yet reconnected to the cloud executor.
  • No profitability is claimed; paper trading only, and live accounts are never connected.
  • The recording hides prices and news from licensed data sources. Some industry dashboards are shown in their labelled sample mode, and the interface is in Chinese.
